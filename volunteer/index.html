What to expect

How volunteering works

  1. Sign up

    Pick a role and a time slot on SignUpGenius, and you’re on the schedule for November 21st.

  2. Show up

    Arrive a little before doors open. We’ll do a quick briefing, get everyone set up at their stations and walk through the flow for the day.

  3. Coach, don’t fix

    Visitors bring the items; you bring the know-how. Guide them through the repair so they leave having actually done it themselves.

  4. Enjoy the room

    Between repairs there’s food, drinks and great conversation. You’ll meet interesting people and leave with a real sense of accomplishment.

Time commitment: roughly 3–4 hours at a single event. No ongoing obligation, though we’d love to have you back.

Fix things with people

Repair coaches

The hands-on repair disciplines we need covered. Your job is to guide visitors through fixing their own items, not to fix things for them.

  • Electronics & small appliances

    Help visitors diagnose and fix lamps, fans, radios, chargers and more.

    More about this role

    Electricians, hobbyists, tinkerers, makers: if you can diagnose a circuit or wield a soldering iron, we need you most. You’ll sit with visitors and help them understand what’s wrong with their lamps, fans, radios, chargers and more, then guide them through the fix step by step.

    Ideal background: electronics repair, electrical engineering, hobbyist maker, appliance repair experience.

  • Sewing & textiles

    Guide visitors through torn seams, missing buttons and frayed hems.

    More about this role

    Whether you’re a professional tailor or just very comfortable with a needle and thread, you’ll help visitors work through torn seams, missing buttons and frayed hems. Bringing your own portable sewing machine is a big help but not required.

    Ideal background: tailoring, seamstress/seamster work, home sewing, costume design, textile arts.

  • Furniture & woodworking

    Steady wobbly chairs, stuck drawers and loose joints.

    More about this role

    Wobbly chairs, broken hinges, stuck drawers, loose joints: you’ll guide visitors through stabilizing, reattaching and restoring furniture they care about. Helping someone bring a beloved piece back to life with their own hands is one of the most rewarding repairs there is.

    Ideal background: woodworking, carpentry, furniture restoration, general handyperson skills.

  • Bicycle repair

    Patch flats, adjust brakes and gears, and get bikes back on the road.

    More about this role

    Bike mechanics, commuters and weekend riders who know their way around a wrench: you’ll help visitors patch flats, adjust brakes and derailleurs, clean and replace chains, and get their bikes back on the road. Bringing your own bike tools or a repair stand is a big help but not required.

    Ideal background: bike shop mechanic, bike co-op volunteer, cycling enthusiast who does their own maintenance.

  • Toys, ceramics, jewelry & general repair

    The catch-all: toys, mugs, jewelry and household odds and ends.

    More about this role

    Broken toys, chipped mugs, cracked ceramics, broken necklaces, loose clasps, household odds and ends: anything that needs a steady hand, a creative problem-solver, or just someone willing to give it a go.

    Ideal background: general handyperson, maker, tinkerer, ceramics, kintsugi, jewelry making or repair, fine craft work.

No repair skills needed

Event support

A Repair Café needs more than repair experts. These roles keep the event running smoothly and make visitors feel welcome.

  • Visitor welcoming & intake

    Greet visitors and check them in with a waiver and intake form.

    More about this role

    The first face visitors see when they walk in the door. You’ll greet people warmly, help them fill out a waiver and intake form describing their item, and point them in the right direction. A calm, friendly presence goes a long way in making the event feel welcoming to first-timers.

  • Event coordination & flow

    Keep the event moving: wait times, stations and logistics.

    More about this role

    Help manage the overall event flow. Keep an eye on wait times, route visitors to the right repair stations, troubleshoot logistics on the fly, and make sure volunteers have what they need. Think of it as air traffic control for a busy repair room.

  • Photography & documentation

    Capture repairs in progress and the spirit of the day.

    More about this role

    Help us capture the spirit of the event: candid shots of repairs in progress, the community atmosphere, and the moment someone’s item comes back to life. These photos are how we show the Peninsula what we’re all about.

Spread the word

Outreach & design

Before we can fix anything, people need to know we exist. We need creative, community-minded folks to help get the word out across the Peninsula.

  • Graphic design

    Create flyers, social graphics and the look of RCP.

    More about this role

    Help us build a visual identity for RCP. Create event flyers, social media graphics and anything else we need to promote events. We’re a scrappy grassroots group, so we lean heavily on volunteers for this. If you have an eye for design and want to leave your mark on what RCP looks like, this is the role for you.

    Ideal background: graphic design, illustration, branding, visual communication. Familiarity with Canva, Figma, Illustrator or similar tools is helpful.

  • Flyer distribution

    Put flyers up in your neighborhood and around the Peninsula.

    More about this role

    Old-fashioned but effective: getting physical flyers up in coffee shops, libraries, laundromats, community centers, grocery store bulletin boards and anywhere else people gather across the Peninsula. If you’re out and about in your neighborhood and willing to carry a stack of flyers, this is incredibly valuable.

    Ideal background: strong local knowledge and a willingness to walk, bike, run, skate or jog around your community.

  • Social media

    Post, share and engage on local community channels.

    More about this role

    Help RCP build a presence on Instagram, Nextdoor, Facebook community groups and other local channels. This could mean creating content, writing posts, engaging with comments, or simply sharing our posts in community groups you’re already part of.

    Ideal background: social media management, content creation, community management, or just being very online.

  • Community partnerships

    Connect us with libraries, schools and community groups.

    More about this role

    Know people at local libraries, schools, community centers, faith organizations or neighborhood associations? Help us build relationships with groups that can host future events, share our announcements, or connect us with volunteers and visitors. This kind of grassroots networking is how we grow.

    Ideal background: people well-connected in their local community or comfortable making introductions.
